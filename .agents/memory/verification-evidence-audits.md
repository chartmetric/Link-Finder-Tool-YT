---
name: Verification evidence audits
description: Distinguish original verifier inputs from reconstructions and synthetic fixtures.
---

No direct channel candidate is not the same as no YouTube evidence.

**Why:** A saved YouTube pilot contained music-video search results while its
channel-only URL parser produced no candidates. Those results had not been
followed to uploader channels; some snippets also mentioned possible handles.

**How to apply:** Audit raw search receipts alongside selected channels.
Distinguish empty searches, video-only discovery gaps, Topic results and
ownership rejections. Video text and handle mentions remain unverified clues,
not captured channel metadata, ownership proof or sourced account absence.

Repeated retrieval of a channel is not proof of competing ownership.

**Why:** A collaborator's channel surfaced for several artists in one saved
test, and the shared-channel guard held every artist/channel pair before
model checking, including the same-named artist's candidate.

**How to apply:** Describe these as unresolved shared-candidate holds, not
confirmed wrong identities or confirmed missed matches. Any future change
must be separately approved, retain explicit ownership conflicts, and keep
assessments bound to each exact artist/channel pair.

A model-readable channel capture is not a replayable provider packet. Rebuild
pre-model comparison inputs with the frozen assembler and original capture,
then require the archived decision hash to match; label held inputs as
reconstructions, not outgoing requests.

**Why:** Normalized captures omit transport status and may omit the raw receipt
required to bind legacy channel aliases. Feeding them back through capture
validation can silently remove otherwise bound channel evidence.

**How to apply:** Preserve the original packet alongside the model-readable
state in offline comparisons. Keep outgoing-request hashes separate from
evidence-state hashes, including transport-added instructions.

Treat lossless source reconstruction and model-readable equivalence as separate
claims. Measure a compact format against the same complete evidence, not only
against historical requests containing bounded summaries.

**Why:** Retaining all saved post records and untruncated metadata can expose
more oversized requests than an earlier context-only audit. Reversible tables
can reduce repetition without making the largest cases fit, and no offline
round trip establishes that a verifier interprets that layout correctly.

**How to apply:** Count dictionary and decoder overhead, report both gains and
remaining holds, preserve frozen inputs, and keep experimental serialization
offline until separately reviewed and approved. Do not discard source records
or increase limits to make the coverage comparison appear better.

For already-frozen byte-held requests, a separately approved, measured larger
allowance is preferable to an experimental encoding when it can preserve the
exact outgoing bodies within the existing spending allocation.

**Why:** A lossless decoder round trip does not prove model-readable equivalence;
unchanged request hashes avoid that additional uncertainty.

**How to apply:** Prepare the smallest allowance covering the full held bodies,
keep it non-executable pending approval, and distinguish archived reservations
from a fresh shared-ledger reconciliation and provider-enforced cost limits.

Affiliation terms in a musician biography do not independently establish that the account is a label, management company or fan account.

**Why:** Saved-batch reviews found musician self-descriptions mentioning label ownership held by the same vocabulary intended to screen out organizational accounts.

**How to apply:** Separate what an account is from what its biography mentions. Assess its role using bound evidence, preserve historical decisions, and do not treat a favorable model answer as independently reviewed ownership.

Name/phrase-overlap audits are review triage, not ownership decisions.

**Why:** Short stage names can appear inside legitimate credited names, and a
biography can state an alias before announcing an unrelated release. Simple
substring and nearby-release-word rules can flag these as title confusion.

**How to apply:** Distinguish actual quoted/release-title contexts from identity
headers, preserve plausible aliases, and keep risk flags separate from confirmed
user corrections. Do not infer missing checker inputs from metadata merely
available elsewhere; absent original model states remain unauditable.
Compare a targeted title-confusion guard with a broader reference requirement
before adoption. A broader gate can hold many plausible matches: more holds do
not demonstrate better accuracy, and an offline hold must not become a mismatch
verdict or an unapproved change to a frozen campaign.

The user approved enabling the targeted title-confusion guard for future runs,
not the broader all-reference gate or the active campaign.

**Why:** They explicitly chose “Enable for future runs” after comparing the
targeted guard with the much broader reference requirement.

**How to apply:** Newly prepared Instagram runs opt into the targeted guard.
Historical results stay unchanged. Resuming an existing frozen campaign is a
continuation, not permission to upgrade its acceptance policy; preserve its
sealed implementation and original model answers.

The user subsequently approved explicit-alias discovery for future runs only.
The separate mention-only review guard remains offline and is not approved
for activation.

**Why:** After reviewing the offline comparison, the user chose “Alias discovery
only,” not “Enable both extensions.” Extra review holds can affect legitimate
aliases, whereas discovery only adds unverified candidates.

**How to apply:** Enable explicit alias extraction from fresh or retained
search provenance when preparing new runs. Derive saved-evidence aliases before
freezing the candidate URL set and spending scope, never inside an already
authorized scope. Keep the active campaign's sealed parser unchanged.
Search aliases remain unverified clues, never captured biographies, ownership
proof, or permission to share one artist's assessment with another artist using
the same handle. Do not enable the mention-only guard without separate approval.

Do not attribute an archived model result to a request reconstructed with a newer serializer. Keep source-path availability, account ownership, model disagreement and application acceptance separate.

**Why:** Historical completion packets can lack outgoing input bytes, while subsequent question changes and provenance repairs change both evidence interpretation and request size. A complete music-ID path does not establish how the historical model received it or prove that a wording repair fixes its score.

**How to apply:** Prefer archived request bodies and hashes when available; explicitly label reconstructions otherwise. Measure the whole serialized request including every candidate and typed question against the existing byte cap. Fixture decisions test the acceptance contract, not model accuracy. When isolating simultaneous verdict and score failures with artificial answers, keep real outputs alongside the counterfactuals; structurally eligible cases are not recovered or verified links. A new paid evaluation always needs its own approval and exclusive journal.

A completed research workflow with no evaluated holdouts has not passed an
ownership quality check.

**Why:** A generic batch can complete an empty regression stage successfully
while every discovered candidate remains unsupported. Operational completion
must not become authorization for attribution corrections or imports.

**How to apply:** Require an explicit scored benchmark record for quality
readiness. Keep research-only workflow completion, candidate discovery,
source-supported attribution and claims eligibility as separate results.

Preserve explicit account declarations and exact alternate-platform catalog
paths ahead of general metadata when fitting verifier requests. A path to the
artist's frozen YouTube, X or TikTok account need not pass through Spotify.
Profile/platform snapshots remain contextual even when they name a handle.

**Why:** Bulk-run reviews exposed plausible accounts held because the verifier
received only a Spotify reference, while corroborating catalog links and saved
declarations were unused or dropped to fit duplicated question text. Finding
candidates and delivering their evidence are separate success criteria.

**How to apply:** Normalize compatibility Unicode in platform-labeled handles;
keep provenance and age; deduplicate prompts before omitting low-priority facts.
Record omissions and hold explicitly if essential evidence cannot fit. Do not
claim archived decisions improved without a separately approved model rerun.

Keep observed search layouts separate from adversarial mutations of saved
results. A locale query or several named artists in a caption is not a
reviewed owner header or evidence of coauthorship.

**Why:** Saved search packets contain localized separators and mixed-account
content, but do not necessarily contain every ownership failure being tested.
Calling derived threat cases real captures would overstate the evidence.

**How to apply:** Preserve scrubbed formatting provenance; label mutations
explicitly and leave unsupported owner layouts unconfirmed. Fixture coverage
alone does not authorize expanding supported owner formats.

Keep repeated artist/account observations distinct from independent sample size.

**Why:** A favorable replay can coexist with another held or conflicting occurrence
for the same account; selecting only that replay hides disagreement and inflates
apparent coverage. Cross-artist account reuse is an unresolved ownership risk,
not an automatic identity adjudication.

**How to apply:** Retain occurrence-level decisions alongside pair-level summaries,
identify the aggregation rule, and report distinct artists, accounts and pairs.
Never describe an offline source-policy acceptance as a fresh model result or a
production-approved correction.

Keep recorded model scores unchanged when an evidence-based research policy
finds additional support. Show the proposed support route separately from the
historical score and verdict.

**Why:** The user found correct candidates held below 0.90. A policy simulation
can change candidate support without changing what the model actually answered;
silently upgrading the saved score or calling that a fresh verification would
misrepresent the experiment.

Keep account-owned post clues distinct from third-party release mentions and
related content, even when saved profile bios are incomplete. A release title
or hashtag match supplies consistency context, never ownership proof.

**Why:** The user requested recovery of omitted post evidence without weakening
the ownership threshold or conflating fans, covers, mashups and collaborators
with the artist.

**How to apply:** Preserve search-source attribution and date uncertainty; do not
describe search snippets as newly fetched posts. Any separately approved model
evaluation must distinguish evidence delivery from ownership accuracy.

Removing prior workflow identity labels is input hygiene, not evidence of
improved recall. Keep source-resolution states and explicit ownership uncertainty
separate from those prior identity judgments.

**Why:** The previous two-artist label-removal experiment did not improve
acceptance; a cleaner request alone does not demonstrate better decisions.

**How to apply:** Exclude workflow identity status/reasons consistently across
normal and compact inputs without rewriting archived requests or decisions.
Do not infer permission for paid reruns, new ownership criteria, threshold
changes or link imports from this hygiene rule.

Historical "facts-only" ablations are not the current source-only contract:
their intentional source-field omissions must remain reproducible. A newly
assembled baseline is not an archived historical baseline.

**Why:** Reusing an old ablation to build fresh inputs removes resolution and
unknown-ownership evidence along with workflow labels, confounding the hygiene
change with a different evidence set.

**How to apply:** Give fresh source-only construction a separate, field-specific
path. Frozen transports must verify and preserve admitted bytes, not sanitize
them after hashing. Label synthetic historical-contract fixtures as synthetic,
not as archived captures.

Missing saved evidence-delivery counts mean unknown, never zero. Per-candidate
scan diagnostics may be taken from original archived request packets, but must
not be recomputed with today's matcher to describe a historical decision.

**Why:** Older pilot archives predate omission diagnostics. A model hold does not
tell us whether its request received all discovered post evidence.

**How to apply:** Show unavailable counts explicitly; distinguish saved result
audits from original request diagnostics and keep both separate from verdicts.

Supplemental saved catalog metadata does not inherit the archived discovery
batch's spending authorization.

**Why:** The user requires verification-only reuse of archived candidates, with
explicit spending approval even when the metadata is already available.

**How to apply:** Prepare offline first, bind new approval to the exact saved
evidence and candidate scope, preserve historical packets and source dates, and
retain non-replayable admissions. Never repeat discovery or enable experimental
acceptance as a side effect of supplementing evidence.

For YouTube evidence, absent Official Artist Channel and auto-generation flags
mean unknown, not false. A generic verified badge is not an Official Artist
Channel badge.

**Why:** Archived provider responses supplied a generic verification field but
omitted both channel-role flags. Defaulting those missing fields to false adds
negative assertions that the source did not make.

**How to apply:** Preserve field presence and uncertainty in future evidence
normalization. Do not infer channel ownership from generic verification, and
do not claim that correcting these inputs improves archived model decisions
without a separately approved comparison.

Reference collection must depend on the platform being investigated. Distinguish
references excluded from collection from references actually absent in the catalog.

**Why:** A YouTube pilot reused an Instagram-oriented metadata collector that
excluded Instagram accounts and snapshots. Zero Instagram references in its
verifier inputs therefore did not establish zero available catalog references.
Successful captures and a larger request allowance could not repair that omission.

**How to apply:** For future YouTube preparation, retain exact catalog-bound
Instagram references without changing Instagram-target collection defaults or
frozen workers. Measure actual delivered inputs separately from upstream catalog
availability, which remains unknown until queried. Existing catalog links are
contextual references, not independently adjudicated ownership or automatic
acceptance.

YouTube alias consolidation is authorized only for newly prepared work, not
archived assessments or active sealed workers. Exact localized Spotify artist
ID agreement is supporting context, never ownership or a resolved channel role.

**Why:** The offline pilot found one channel checked under two addresses with
likely verdicts but unknown roles. Fixing duplicate work and URL comparison must
not turn those held observations into newly accepted matches or refund charges.

**How to apply:** Consolidate only provider-bound aliases within the same catalog
artist and channel ID. Preserve every source capture and existing reservation;
keep differing observations and shared-channel conflicts on hold. Any paid
recheck or continuation still needs separate approval.
